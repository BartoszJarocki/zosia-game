# Pup Patrol review, November 2026

Zosia is 3.5 and has outgrown the game. Her parent reported three things on 2026-11-10: she finds it easy, some levels are barely playable and make little sense, and the UI and bugs get in the way everywhere. This review confirms all three, with evidence, and explains why earlier checks reported the same build as verified. The plan that follows from it is `docs/IMPROVEMENT-PLAN.md`.

Every finding is labelled **measured** (run in a real browser or computed from a run), **verified** (read in the code by the reviewer who signs this), **reported** (found by a code reviewer and not re-checked), or **inferred**. The build is `main` at c5ff849, the one deployed on 2026-11-08.

## The short version

- **Easy.** Every place starts at the very beginning, whatever she already knows. The top levels stop at about what a four-year-old does. In 15 of the 25 missions the answer is on screen at some or all levels, or the first wrong tap shows it. A visit is three or four questions.
- **Barely playable.** In the counting games a quick child's taps are dropped: three taps on the lighthouse lamp counted as one, and the game answered "Za mało!". Three of the four sea games are hard to read on screen, and one level needs her to read.
- **UI.** At the medium and high graphics settings every star on the island is a blurred glow and the top of the island is out of focus. A fast iPad runs one of those two. Earlier reviews ran on the lowest setting, where the island is crisp.
- **Waiting.** After a right answer she waits 4.3 seconds for the next question in the middle case, and more than 8 seconds one time in four. A trip to space takes 26 seconds before she can pick a planet, every time.
- **The session works against her.** A child who makes no mistakes loses a third of her "attention score" by moving to a slower kind of game. After that, looking away three times ends the session and puts the island to sleep.

## How this review was done

Earlier reviews drove the game with a polite bot on a software renderer at the lowest graphics setting (`?q=low`). The bot waited for the voice to finish, tapped the right answer once, and passed a level when the celebration appeared. That check cannot see what the iPad draws, what a fast tap does, how long she waits, or whether a question can be answered without the skill. It passed 123 of 124 levels on 2026-11-06 and 14 of 14 sea levels on 2026-11-08.

This review changed four things.

1. **The real graphics.** Headless Chromium runs on this laptop's GPU with `--use-angle=gl-egl`. The game runs at `?q=high`, with the bloom and tilt-shift passes. The automatic setting starts at medium and moves to high on a fast device, and both run those passes (`src/fx/post.js`). A slow iPad can drop to low, where they are off. Which one her iPad settles on is not known.
2. **A child-like player.** `tools/playtest/kid.mjs` taps by touch. Its `fast` plan answers the moment an answer can be tapped. Its `mash` plan pokes ten things quickly first, including two fingers at once.
3. **Every level.** The `fast` plan played all 138 levels of the 25 missions. The `mash` plan played the first and the top level of each.
4. **Six code reviews.** Six reviewers read every scene, the island, the maps and the session logic for defects the happy path hides. The findings marked **verified** were re-read in the code. Five were reproduced in a browser.

One limit remains. Nothing here ran on an iPad. Sound, touch, speed and the graphics setting on the device are inferred. Three photos from the iPad would settle the last one.

## Why she finds it easy

### Every place starts at the beginning

A mission's level is saved per mission, and a new mission starts at level 1 (`src/progress.js`, `mission()`). Nothing carries over. A child at Cone Road level 9, who counts ten dots, starts Mercury with one to three craters, the numeral and its dots on every tile (GAME.md, Mercury level 1). The same holds for the Moon, Mars, the Black Hole, the Lighthouse and the Stars. **Verified.**

Moving up takes two good runs per level (`goodRunsPerLevel: 2`). There are 138 levels in 25 missions, so reaching the top everywhere takes 113 level-ups, which is 226 clean runs. A session suggests two missions and then the Lookout ride, which does not count (`lookout.js:51`). **Verified** from the tables. How many sessions she plays is unknown.

### The top levels stop at about age four

| Area | What the hardest level asks |
|---|---|
| Numbers | Count 7 to 10 dots and find the numeral (Cone Road 9). Give exactly 8 to 10 treats (Treat House 8). What comes after 6 (River 7). Count on 1 to 3 floors (Lookout 8). |
| Letters | Find a letter by its picture word among 4 with lookalikes (Kitty Park 10). Put in 2 missing letters (Name Train 8). First sound to letter (Delivery 5). Syllables 1 to 3 (Port 2). |
| Shapes and logic | Circle, square, triangle, turned (Garage, Venus). The pattern ABC (Jupiter 4). Sort by colour, then by size (Garage 7). |

Nothing goes past 10. There is no adding or taking away beyond "one more". There are no lowercase letters, no syllable reading, and no shapes beyond three and a heart. In October the plan held back "numbers to 10 everywhere" as a guess about her. The parent's report says that guess was wrong for her.

## Bugs that hit a fast child

- **Dropped taps.** Lighthouse lamp, Moon jump, the next star for 1.2 s, the next Name Train letter for about 2.5 s after each right one, the right tile on Mercury for about 4 s after a wrong one, Venus after a wrong tap, the Lookout's floor buttons while the die waits. None gives a sound or a movement. **Measured** for the first two, **reported** for the rest.
- **Double landings.** A finger that lands twice 70 ms apart gives two treats in Treat House, and in the share round it feeds a friend and then says "Nie, ten już ma!" (**reported**). The filter in `src/input/bounce.js` only catches a repeated wrong answer, and the Lighthouse defeats even that by overwriting `lastWrong` (`lighthouse.js:407`, **verified**).
- **Stray taps.** A tap that hits nothing goes to the nearest thing within 14% of the screen's short side, about 115 px (`engine.js`). On the island a tap on the sea near the lighthouse started a drive to the Treat House (**measured**, `tour/island`).
- **Scoring leaks.** Floats or sinks never scores a wrong guess. The gentler Comet run has only right comets and counts as a clean run. The gentler runs of Treat House 1 to 3, Picnic 1 to 3 and Name Train 7 are identical to the normal run. **Measured** for the first, **reported** for the rest.
- **The voice.** A new line always cuts the one before it. The fast child cut 19% of all spoken clips short (558 of 2,812) and the mashing child 40% (721 of 1,791), up to 40 of 52 in Saturn (**measured**). Tapping the bubble after a right answer cuts the praise and counts as "asked to hear it again" in the tiredness score. In the Black Hole lines queue: four taps on the speaker play the prompt four times, about 14 s, before her pebble is counted (`blackhole.js:133`, **verified**).
- **No crash found.** The `fast` plan finished all 138 levels and the `mash` plan finished all 50 of its runs, with no page error and no stuck level. A crash inside a scene would leave the blue paw screen up for good, because `pawWipe` has no recovery (`src/fx/transition.js`, **reported**).

## The island, the maps and the trips

**The island at the iPad's setting.** `after-review-2026-11/island-tiers.jpg` shows the same island at low, medium and high. At low the stars are crisp. At medium and high each star is a soft yellow blob, because the stars glow above the bloom threshold, and the top quarter of the picture is blurred by the tilt-shift pass. The rocket, the harbour and the lighthouse sit in that quarter. Under every place hangs a row of up to ten small level stars. In portrait the suggested star is a white smear over the Name Train, and the rows of level stars cross the Lookout and the Garage. **Measured.**

**Twelve places, one look.** The suggested place differs from the others by size and a halo that the bloom washes out. Tapping Chase on arrival cuts the invitation before "Może do… Dotknij gwiazdki!", and it returns only after 25 s (**reported**).

**Trips.** From the tap on the rocket to a map she can tap: 26 seconds, every trip. START appears after 17 s (**measured**, `probes/maphome`). The boat takes 20 s. There is no way back during the countdown. From Earth to the star pictures is 19 s, with "Mijamy…" at each planet, and taps on another planet during the flight do nothing (**measured**, `probes/farflight`). Sailing home from the port passes every stop and takes 18 s (**measured**).

**Leaving by accident.** The house button needs a 0.8 s hold in a mission and a single tap on the maps (`ui.js:138`). One tap on the map sent her to the island in 6 s, and coming back costs the 26 s launch again (**measured**). Earth on the space map and the island on the bay map also mean "go home", and they look like any other stop.

**The bay map.** The stops are small on a wide empty sea: the port is a cluster about 40 px wide and the seal's rock reads as a dark blob (`tour/sea`, **measured**). Game stops and look-only stops look the same on both maps.

**Updates.** The app asks for a new version once per page load. An iPad home-screen app that was only suspended keeps the old build until it is closed and reopened, and the build label shows only on the start screen (`main.js:59`, **reported**, iPad behaviour **inferred**). Some of what the parent saw may come from an older build.

## The session logic works against her

The tiredness score (`src/session/attention.js`) compares her recent answers with the first four of the session. It does not know that a Treat House answer is many taps and a Kitty Park answer is one. Fed with realistic play (`tools/playtest/attention-sim.mjs`, **measured** with the game's own module):

| Play | Score | State |
|---|---|---|
| Kitty Park, then Treat House, no mistake at all | 0.70 | ok, 0.10 above "tired" |
| The same, and she taps the bubble once a round | 0.60 | on the edge |
| The same, excited: two bursts of taps, one look away | 0.39 | tired: the finale comes next and nothing new is asked |
| The same, and she looks away three times | 0.33 | finish: "Jeszcze tylko jedno!", goodnight |

After the goodnight the island sleeps. Every place answers "Czejs śpi… Do jutra!" (**measured**, `probes/asleep`). It wakes 15 minutes after her last answer, or when someone holds a place for 1.5 s, which nothing on screen says. Chase himself keeps saying he is asleep the next morning until a place is tapped (**reported**). Pressing "again" on the celebration after a wind-down gives two-round missions that each announce "Jeszcze tylko jedno!" (**reported**).

## What my earlier checks could not see

The last two releases were reported as verified. The check behind that word was too weak in four ways, and all four are fixed in the plan's first unit.

1. It ran at the lowest graphics setting. The glow, the blur and the washed-out pale scenes do not exist there.
2. It waited for the voice and tapped once. Dropped taps, double landings and long waits never happened to it.
3. It passed a level when the celebration appeared. A level whose answer is in the bubble passes that test perfectly.
4. It ran on a software renderer where one level took five minutes, so nobody watched a whole mission at real speed. On the GPU, Kitty Park takes 27 seconds.

## What is good and should stay

- The island missions look finished at the iPad's setting: Kitty Park, the Name Train, the Treat House and the Lookout read clearly and their models are large (`fast/letters-L1`).
- The space map is clear and handsome, and the launch is a good first-time moment.
- Mercury's quick-look levels work as designed.
- The voice, the kind "no", and the rule that nothing can be lost are right for her.
- No run crashed.
